# ADR-086 — the AI execution layer, bounded to observation before routing (link stub)

**Authoritative copy:** [`exeris-docs/adr/ADR-086-bound-the-ai-execution-layer-to-observation-before-routing.md`](https://github.com/exeris-systems/exeris-docs/blob/main/adr/ADR-086-bound-the-ai-execution-layer-to-observation-before-routing.md)

ADR-086 fixes what this layer may do before it may decide: V0 observes and does not route (§B.5), the
seam runs from `exeris-agents` to here and never the other way (§A.2), a run record carries metadata
and never the material it was derived from (§C.12), and paired runs — not ambient rows — are what can
support a routing claim (§E.22).

## Why this stub exists

This repository **is** the layer the ADR bounds. §A.1 gives it the workload model, the event store,
the dataset, the oracle interface and everything downstream, and what is here today is the beginning
of that: `schemas/run-record.schema.json` and `schemas/judgement-record.schema.json` are §C's contract
in machine form, `inbox/` is the landing zone §F and §G describe, and `tools/inbox_validate.py`
enforces the cross-file rules of §G.34 that no single schema can express.

A reader who finds a schema here and asks why a row may not carry a prompt, why an uncalibrated
oracle cannot produce a passing outcome, or why nothing in this repository aggregates anything, is
asking about ADR-086. The last one is the easiest to undo by accident: a script that compares two
rows would be a `[HARD BLOCK]` under §A.2 whatever its size.

Engineering Protocol 6 of the ADR owes this stub; it is paid here.
